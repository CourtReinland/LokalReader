The Quiet Carriage

Chapter 1
The rain stitched silver threads against the carriage window. The train leaned into a long curve, and the lamp overhead swayed like a cautious thought.

"Do you always travel with that many books?" asked Mara, nodding at the stack beside him.

Eli smiled without looking up from the page. "Only when I intend to finish none of them," he said. "It keeps my intentions honest."

Mara laughed, soft enough not to wake the sleeping porter. "Then you're the most honest man on this train."

"Hardly," Eli replied. "Honesty is just another genre. Some people prefer mystery."

She leaned closer, lowering her voice. "And what do you prefer tonight?"

"Silence," he whispered, "until the story insists otherwise."

Outside, the station lights blurred past like hurried footnotes. Mara opened her own book, then closed it again.

"Eli," she said, "listen. If the next stop is Hollowmere, I'm getting off."

"Hollowmere isn't on the timetable," Eli answered. "Which is precisely why you want it."

The porter stirred. "Tickets," he muttered, more dream than duty.

Mara: "We have them."
Eli: "And we have time — or we don't. Either way, keep reading."

The carriage settled into its rhythm again: rail joints, rain, the small courage of shared quiet. Somewhere ahead, the night prepared another chapter.
